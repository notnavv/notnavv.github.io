What it does

Most poker apps let you play. Bullets teaches you while you play: after every hand, each decision is graded with the equity and pot-odds math behind it, so you know what to do differently next time.

  • Freeplay against six AI opponent styles, in Hold'em and Omaha
  • 55 lessons with 30 playable scenarios, plus daily puzzles
  • Real-time online rooms, public matchmaking and friends with live presence
  • An Elo rating, rank ladder and leaderboards
  • Hand replays with every decision graded

How it's built

The hand-analysis engine grades each decision with Monte Carlo equity and pot-odds math. The app is React and TypeScript, with Supabase (Postgres, Auth and Realtime) running online rooms, matchmaking, friends and leaderboards. It ships to iOS with Capacitor and is deployed on Vercel.

Decisions worth asking about

01

Rating only moves at four or more players

Heads-up and three-handed hands don't change your rating, so it reflects real table play instead of easy spots.

02

No odds display during live hands

Freeplay and online tables don't show live equity. You make the call on your own, and the math comes after the hand, in the review.

03

Grade the decision, not the result

A hand you lost can still get a good grade. The feedback is about whether each move was right given what you knew, not how the cards fell.
